# What I practiced during the course

## Organizing data and calculating meaningful summaries

I practiced distinguishing numeric fields from categories, organizing observations by year or group, and using Excel to calculate counts, averages, standard deviations, and frequencies. Passenger records, coin-mass measurements, and annual employment ratios require different summaries. The exercises gave me concrete practice distinguishing an individual observation, a group percentage, and a total across years.

## Choosing a chart for the question

Line charts helped me compare several employment groups over time. Frequency charts in the coin and Titanic exercises showed the distribution of measurements, ages, or fares. Bar charts supported comparisons of groups and gaps. The settlement-time exercise represented the same process-stage data with different charts, giving me a way to compare how clearly each chart showed the overall total and the relative size of its components.

## Connecting the calculation to the comparison

I practiced moving from raw values to differences and proportions: subtracting the bachelor’s-degree ratio from the advanced-degree ratio, or comparing men and women within the same year. The archive also gives a concrete example of why the denominator matters. The share of survivors who travelled first class is different from the survival rate among first-class passengers. These exercises provide a basis for checking years, units, denominators, and aggregation when revisiting an analysis.

## Explaining the analysis to an audience

The final project connected spreadsheet work with oral explanation. I practiced introducing a question, showing a chart, identifying an observation, and explaining how that observation related to the question. Bilingual rehearsal notes and a collaborative presentation also involved dividing speaking sections, connecting the parts of the talk, and describing groups, time ranges, and changes in English. The saved work documents that practice while leaving historical interpretations open to further checking.
