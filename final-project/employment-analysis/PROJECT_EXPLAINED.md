# Final project Comparing U.S. employment patterns

## What the project presents

This project organizes U.S. employment data into charts that let an audience compare changes across age groups, education levels, and gender. The completed work includes an Excel file with seven worksheets, a 22-slide presentation, and bilingual speaking notes. Overall trends, selected close-ups, and difference charts let viewers follow the comparisons without reading the original table row by row.

## From the table to the presentation

The workbook retains 44 annual rows labelled 1979–2022 and 122 fields. Separate worksheets organize age, education, gender, and related subgroup comparisons. The age section compares people aged 16–24, 25–54, 55–64, and 65+. The education section compares five categories and calculates the advanced-degree minus bachelor’s-degree gap. The gender section compares men and women, including differences within age groups. Line charts show changes over time, bar charts compare groups, and close-ups support discussion of selected periods.

## An example of the comparison

In the saved education table, the advanced-degree group is 82.8% in 1979 and the bachelor’s-degree group is 75.0%, a difference of 7.8 percentage points. The saved difference for 2015 is 0.3 points. Plotting the yearly difference separately makes the distance between the two groups easier to inspect than comparing two lines alone. This describes the table’s pattern; it does not establish that educational expansion or a particular policy caused the change.
